Privacy Policy

Lantern City

Effective Date: 21 October 2026

Trinath Sai Subhash Reddy Pittala ("we", "us", or "our") built Lantern City as a free rhythm game for everyone, children included. This Privacy Policy explains what the app stores, what it does not do, and what the one third-party library in it may receive. The Terms of Service for the game follow it on this page.

1. In one sentence

Lantern City itself does not collect, store, share or sell any personal information. Two things in it talk to the internet: the ad library, which shows short ads between stages, chosen according to the age you gave (never personalised for a child or a teen), and, only if you choose to sign in, Google Play Games (on Android) or Apple Game Center (on iPhone and iPad), which keeps your achievements and total scores on your own profile there.

2. What the app stores

Lantern City keeps a few small things on your device only: your progress (which stages you have cleared, your best scores and ranks), your settings (whether music, sound effects and vibration are on), and the age group your answer fell in when the game first opened (child, teen or adult; never the age itself).

That information never leaves the device. It is not backed up by us, not synced, not sent to any server, and not visible to anyone else. Deleting the app deletes it. The "Reset progress" option in Settings wipes your progress too.

3. What the app does not do

No account is needed and nothing asks you to sign in; the optional Google Play Games or Game Center sign-in is described below, and the game is complete without it. No name, email address, exact age, location, contacts, photos, microphone or camera. No analytics, no crash reporting, no purchases and no subscriptions, and the game itself tracks nothing (what an adult may allow the ad network is under Ads).

The app asks for no permissions, except one question on iPhone and iPad, put only to an adult: Apple's prompt asking whether the ad network may use the device's advertising identifier. Apart from the ads, the optional Play Games or Game Center sign-in described below, and the later islands, which the app store delivers the first time each is opened, it makes no network requests of its own.

4. Ads

The whole game is free. To keep it that way, a short ad may play when you move on from a stage you have cleared, once you have cleared a few. An ad never plays during a stage or on the title screen. Ads are shown by Google AdMob, one of the ad networks Google Play certifies for apps that children use.

When the game first opens it asks your age, without suggesting an answer, and keeps only the group it falls in; until it is answered, the game treats the player as a child. For a child (under 13), every ad request is marked as directed at children and under the age of consent, is capped at G-rated content and asks for non-personalised ads only, and there is no consent form and no tracking prompt. For a teen (13 to 17), requests are marked as under the age of consent, capped at PG and non-personalised, again with no consent form and no tracking prompt.

For an adult (18 and over), ads may be rated up to T (teen) and may be personalised, but only if you agree: where the law asks for consent (for example in the European Economic Area and the UK), Google asks you first, and on iPhone and iPad Apple asks whether the ad network may use your device's advertising identifier. Saying no still gets you the whole game. Where it applies, Settings has an Ad choices ticket to change your answer later; the advertising identifier can also be switched off in the device's own settings.

Like any ad library, AdMob may receive technical details about the device in order to serve and count an ad: for example the device model, operating system version, language, screen size, a coarse location from the network, identifiers used to limit how often an ad is shown, and, for an adult who has allowed it, the device's advertising identifier. Google's own privacy policy at policies.google.com/privacy describes what it does with that information. We never see it, and we do not collect anything ourselves.

5. Google Play Games (optional)

From the Hall of Records you can sign in to Google Play Games with a Google account. It is optional: nothing in the game needs it, and nothing prompts for it. If you do sign in, Google Play Games records the achievements you earn and your two total scores (story and DJ mode) on your Play Games profile, so they appear on Google's achievements and leaderboards screens and follow you to another device. The app receives your Play Games display name and player id in return and uses them for nothing else; it does not store them.

That profile is Google's, covered by Google's privacy policy at policies.google.com/privacy. You can remove this game's data from it at any time in the Google Play Games app, under Settings, Delete Play Games account and data. On a child's account a parent may have Play Games switched off; then the sign-in ticket does nothing and the game carries on unchanged.

6. Game Center (optional, iPhone and iPad)

On iPhone and iPad the Hall of Records offers Apple's Game Center instead of Google Play Games. It is optional: nothing in the game needs it, and the game never asks. If the device is already signed in to Game Center, Game Center may show its own small welcome banner when the game starts; nothing else happens unless you choose. If you sign in, Game Center records the achievements you earn and your two total scores (story and DJ mode) on your Game Center profile, so they appear on Apple's Game Center screens and follow you to your other Apple devices. The app receives your Game Center display name in return and uses it for nothing else; it does not store it.

That profile is Apple's, covered by Apple's privacy policy at apple.com/privacy. You can sign out of Game Center or manage its data at any time in the Settings app, under Game Center. On a child's device a parent may have Game Center restricted with Screen Time; then the sign-in ticket does nothing and the game carries on unchanged.

7. Children

Lantern City is for everyone, and children play it, so a child gets what a children's app should: ads in the child-directed mode the ad network provides for exactly this (G-rated, never personalised, no consent form, no tracking prompt), nothing to buy, no accounts to create, no chat, no user-generated content, no way to contact other people (Play Games and Game Center leaderboards show only the display names of players who chose to sign in), and no links out to the web except whatever a child might tap inside an ad, which the ad network keeps to child-appropriate content.

The age question is neutral: it suggests no answer, and the game plays the same whatever is answered; only the ads differ. The one thing that reaches outside the game is the Share ticket, offered after a headliner is beaten or a top rank is earned: it opens the device's own share sheet with a link to the game, and the game sees nothing of what happens there. For a child, a grown-up has to answer a question before the share sheet opens.

8. Sound and vibration

The app plays music and sound effects and, on phones, a light vibration on a catch. All three can be switched off in Settings. None of this involves recording anything.

9. Changes

If this policy ever changes, the new version will ship inside the app under Settings and on this page, with the date above updated. Because the app collects so little, we do not expect it to change much.

10. Contact

Questions about this policy, or about your data: trinathpittala@gmail.com. We hold nothing about you, so there is nothing of ours to delete; deleting the app removes everything it kept on the device. What the ad network may hold is Google's: it protects that information under its own privacy policy (policies.google.com/privacy), and you can see and delete it, and turn off ad personalisation, at myadcenter.google.com. Write to us and we will help you find your way there.
