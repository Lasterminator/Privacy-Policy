Privacy Policy

Infinite Labyrinth

Effective Date: October 11, 2026

Trinath Sai Subhash Reddy Pittala ("we", "us", or "our") built Infinite Labyrinth, a free maze game for all ages. This policy explains what the game stores, what it shares, and how to delete it.

1. In short

Infinite Labyrinth works fully offline and keeps your progress on your device. It has no ads, no in-app purchases, no analytics and no crash reporting, and we run no servers of our own. The only thing that goes online is the optional Google Play Games sign-in, described in section 3.

2. What the game stores on your device

Using the device's app storage, the game keeps:

  • Your progress: Journey stars, gems and power-ups, Daily Maze results, challenges, and an Endless run you saved to continue later
  • Your Endless high scores (if you were signed in to Play Games, each entry also notes your Play Games name and player ID)
  • Your settings (music, sound, vibration, run style) and whether you have seen the tutorial
  • A random number the game creates, used only to combine your gem counts correctly when your progress is synced between devices

None of this is sent to us. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the game deletes it.

3. Google Play Games (optional)

On Android the game can use Google Play Games. When the game starts, Play Games may sign you in or ask you once; you can say no, and every part of the game still works. If you sign in:

  • Leaderboards: your Endless score and your Daily Maze times (labelled with the day) are sent to the Play Games leaderboards, where other players can see them next to your Play Games name.
  • Cloud saves: your progress (section 2) and any saved Endless run are stored with Play Games Saved Games, in a hidden app folder in your Google Drive that only this game can read, so they follow you to another device.
  • Your profile: the game receives your Play Games display name and player ID, shows your name in the game, and uses the ID only to keep your scores and saves together.

This data is held by Google under Google's Privacy Policy. You can remove it at any time in the Google Play Games app (Settings, Delete Play Games account and data). On a child's account a parent may have Play Games turned off; the game then simply plays offline.

4. What the game does not do

  • No ads, no purchases, no analytics, no tracking, no crash reporting
  • No chat, no messages, no user-made content, and no way to contact other players
  • No location, contacts, photos, files, microphone or camera
  • No advertising ID or other device identifiers
  • We never sell your data; nothing leaves your device except what goes to Google Play Games when you sign in

5. Children

Infinite Labyrinth is suitable for everyone, including young children. It needs no account, and the optional Play Games sign-in is the only online feature. On leaderboards other players see only a Play Games name and avatar with a score or time.

6. Older versions (before 2.5)

Versions of the game before 2.5 used Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore) for their leaderboard instead of Google Play Games, storing scores with an anonymous identifier generated by the app. To have that data deleted, see our Request data deletion page.

7. Your choices and deleting your data

  • Data on your device: clear the app's storage or uninstall the game.
  • Play Games data: delete it in the Google Play Games app, or never sign in.
  • Anything else, or questions: email us (below), and see the Request data deletion page.

For the app's Terms of Service, see our Terms of Service page.
