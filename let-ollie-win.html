Privacy Policy

Let Ollie Win

Effective Date: November 13, 2026

Trinath Sai Subhash Reddy Pittala ("we", "us", or "our") built Let Ollie Win, a free card puzzle game paid for by ads. This policy explains what the game stores, what it shares, and how to delete it.

1. In short

Let Ollie Win keeps your progress on your device and nowhere else. It shows full-screen ads through Google AdMob; those ads are personalised only if you agree. It has no accounts, no sign-in, no in-app purchases, no leaderboards, no analytics and no crash reporting, and we run no servers of our own.

2. What the game stores on your device

Using the device's app storage, the game keeps:

  • Your progress on each table: how many runs you have used, whether you solved it, and the fewest rule cards you solved it with
  • Your run of days on Today's Table, the days a sick note covered, and which weekly biscuit tins you have opened
  • Your Family Rulebook (which rule cards you have found and how often you solved with each) and Ollie's dances album
  • How many tables you have finished, which the game uses to decide when an ad may show
  • Which story notes and hints you have already seen

Google's consent tool (section 3) also keeps your ad choices on the device. None of this is sent to us. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the game deletes it from the device.

3. Ads

The game is free because of ads, all of them full-screen and none of them on a table while the cards are playing. An ad may play when you leave a finished table (never before your fifth finished table, and never during a run), and you can choose to watch an ad for a reward: one more run when your runs are gone, Grandpa's whisper, a sick note that saves your run of days, or a second biscuit tin in a week. If no ad is available, you get the reward anyway. Ads are shown by Google AdMob and are limited to content rated for general audiences.

Where the law asks for consent (the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland, and some US states), Google's consent form asks you before the first ad. On iPhone, Apple then asks whether the game may use your device's advertising identifier. Ads are personalised only if you agree; if you don't, you still get the whole game, with ads that are not based on your interests. Where it applies, the bottom of the Fridge screen has Ad choices to change your answer at any time, and you can also reset or turn off the advertising identifier in your device's settings.

To show and count ads, AdMob may receive information about the device and your use of the ads: for example the device model, operating system and language, your IP address and a coarse location derived from it, how you interact with the ads, and, if you allowed it, the device's advertising identifier. Google handles this under Google's Privacy Policy (see also how Google uses information from apps that use its services). We do not receive it.

4. Sharing a table, a result or a clip

Nothing leaves the game unless you choose to share it. When you do, your device's own share sheet opens and you pick where it goes.

  • A result: a short text with the table's name, its goal, how many runs you used and a row of coloured squares. It does not say which rule cards you used.
  • A table code, link or QR code: a code such as LOW-W3-7K2QX that only describes the cards on a table (which game, how hard, and a number the cards are dealt from). It contains nothing about you or your device. A friend's phone rebuilds the same table from it without contacting us or anyone else.
  • A clip (iPhone): if you tap "Save a clip", iOS asks for permission and the game records its own screen, without sound from the microphone, while one run replays. The video stays in the game's temporary storage on your device (the next clip replaces it), and it goes only where you send it.

5. What the game does not do

  • No accounts, no sign-in, no leaderboards and no cloud saves
  • No purchases, no analytics, no crash reporting, and no servers of our own
  • No chat, no messages, and no way to contact other players from inside the game
  • No access to your location, contacts, photos, files or camera, and no recording from the microphone
  • We never sell your data, and we never receive what the ad network collects

6. Children

Let Ollie Win is meant for players aged 13 and over and is not directed at children under 13, although the story is about two children playing cards. The game itself collects no personal information from anyone; the ads work as described above.

7. Your choices and deleting your data

  • Data on your device: clear the app's storage or uninstall the game. That removes everything the game has kept.
  • Ads: change your answer with Ad choices on the Fridge screen (where it applies), or reset or turn off the advertising identifier in your device's settings.
  • Anything you shared: it is in the app or chat you sent it to, and is deleted there.
  • Questions: email us (below). We hold no data about you, so there is nothing for us to delete on our side.
